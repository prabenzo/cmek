Killswitch connecting…

Customer-managed encryption keys (CMEK) let each tenant own the key that protects its data: revoke the key and the provider can no longer read it. This is a multi-tenant webhook queue where every message is sealed under a per-tenant data key that is itself wrapped by that tenant's KMS key, leased for at most 30 s.

The thesis: one tenant's KMS trouble, revocation or traffic surge changes nothing for the other 999, and no plaintext is ever decrypted after a key is revoked. Below, 1,000 tenants across three simulated KMS providers run at about 300 events/s; each card injects a fault or a surge and says what to watch.

This is one shared live system: anyone viewing this page sees the same World, and a scenario started here runs for everyone. Traffic runs only while someone is watching.

Slow KMS

One provider, azure, becomes slow enough that many of its calls run past the timeout, for a minute. What happens to the rest of the service depends entirely on where the key fetch runs, and the three buttons let you switch that design live, before or during the run. Naive. No lease, no cache, no bulkheads: every event and every delivery makes its own KMS call and waits for it. The slow provider's calls hold the shared delivery workers, so delivery collapses for every tenant, not just azure's, and the KMS call rate explodes. Sync. The cache and the lease are back, so calls are rare again and the bulkhead caps how many are in flight. But the worker that finds a lease due still fetches inline and waits, and every tenant's deliveries queue behind those waits: throughput mostly survives, latency does not. Async. The design that ships. A lease due for renewal is refreshed in the background while deliveries keep using the cached key, so azure's slowness stays azure's problem: its cells flicker yellow, the healthy line stays flat, and the moment you switch to it the backlog drains. Reset world starts over in Async.

Provider outage

gcp fast-fails. Blip (10 s): up to a third of the grid turns yellow as renewals fail, a few cold cells go orange with one 503, then everything is green within ~12 s of the clear; no chart moves. Outage (60 s): yellow spreads over 0–15 s with no customer impact; from ~9 s leases lapse at staggered times, cells turn orange and those tenants get 503 key_unavailable. aws and azure stay flat. Calls to gcp decay through backoff. On restore, green within ~10 s and the parked messages deliver.

Key revocation

The KMS key of one high-traffic tenant (rank 3) is disabled. Its cell turns purple, typically within 15 s and always within 30 s; ingest for it returns 403 key_revoked; the timeline shows REVOKED and the data keys purged; nothing of its is decrypted after that. Click Restore: the re-probe sees it within 5 s and the parked messages deliver.

Tenant surge

One top-20 tenant (rank 5) sends 100× its rate for 60 s. Its cell stays green, marked with a ring. After the 200-event burst it gets 429 rate_limited above 100 events/s; those 429s show as within share by design. Its KMS calls stay flat: renewals at most 1 per 15 s, plus one data-key rotation per 10,000 accepted messages. Other tenants' p99 does not move.

Global surge

Every tenant sends 5× for 90 s: about 1,500 events/s against a delivery capacity near 640. Delivered holds at capacity instead of collapsing. The heaviest tenants are held to their fair share with 429s and, near 75 s, the heaviest ~40 are shed with 503 overloaded while the other ~960 notice nothing. Backlog levels off under its caps and drains in about a minute. KMS calls stay under ~67/s.
